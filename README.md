## Method

### Model

Cytotoxic killing is represented as a continuous-time stochastic process. Each killer cell generates target-contact events, and each contact results in either a kill or a non-kill outcome.

**State (per killer cell $i$)**  
- $x_i$: cumulative number of non-lethal contacts  
- $y_i$: cumulative number of lethal contacts  
- $K_i$: killing capacity (maximum number of kills available); remaining capacity $K_i^{\mathrm{rem}}$ decreases by 1 after each kill  

**Event generation (global Gillespie step)**  
- Each alive killer has an encounter rate $\lambda_i = r(x_i, y_i)$ (default: constant rate matrix).  
- Next event time is sampled as $\Delta t \sim \mathrm{Exp}\!\left(\sum_i \lambda_i\right)$.  
- The killer responsible for the next event is chosen with probability $\Pr(i\ \text{chosen}) = \lambda_i / \sum_j \lambda_j$.  

**Target depletion (finite target pool)**  
- The initial target count is $T_0$, set either explicitly or as a multiple of killers (default: $T_0 = 4\,N_{\mathrm{killers}}$).  
- Remaining targets $T$ decrease by 1 after each successful kill.  
- Encounter rates are scaled by a depletion factor $f(T) = \max\!\left(T/T_0,\ f_{\min}\right)$, implemented as $\lambda_i \leftarrow \lambda_i\, f(T)$, where `f_min` corresponds to `target_rate_floor`.  

**Decision rule at each contact**  
- Deterministic mode: $p_{\mathrm{kill}}(x_i,y_i)=1$ while $K_i^{\mathrm{rem}}>0$; otherwise $p_{\mathrm{kill}}=0$.  
- Stochastic mode: $p_{\mathrm{kill}}(x_i,y_i)=p(x_i,y_i)$ from a probability matrix:  
  - Constant: $p(x,y)=p_0$.  
  - History-dependent (logistic): $\mathrm{logit}\,p(x,y)=\mathrm{logit}(p_0)+\alpha x+\beta y$, with $p(x,y)=\sigma(\mathrm{logit}\,p(x,y))$, where $\sigma(\cdot)$ is the sigmoid (ensuring $p\in(0,1)$ up to numerical clipping).  

**Killing capacity $K_i$ generation**  
- Homogeneous: $K_i = K_0$ for all killers.  
- Heterogeneous (continuous): $K_i$ sampled from Normal / Lognormal / Gamma distributions and rounded to integers.  
- Heterogeneous (discrete mixture): $K_i$ sampled from a mixture of communities with specified proportions and community-specific capacities.  
- Optional clipping to $[K_{\min}, K_{\max}]$.  

**Outputs**  
- Per-killer decision sequences (0 = non-lethal, 1 = lethal) and corresponding event times  
- Final $(x_i, y_i)$, capacities $K_i$, remaining capacities $K_i^{\mathrm{rem}}$  
- Target trajectory $T(t)$ evaluated at event times  



---
**Description**
This simulator models cytotoxic killing as a continuous-time stochastic process in which multiple killer cells generate target-contact events and, at each contact, decide whether the interaction is lethal or non-lethal. Each killer keeps track of its cumulative non-lethal contacts and lethal kills, and is assigned a finite killing capacity that limits how many kills it can perform before becoming incapable of killing. Contact events are scheduled using a global Gillespie step: at any moment, all alive killers contribute an encounter rate (from a rate matrix that can depend on their contact history), these rates are summed to determine the waiting time to the next event, and the killer responsible for that event is sampled in proportion to its rate. When an event occurs, the chosen killer attempts a kill with a probability given by a killing-probability matrix: this can be deterministic (always killing until capacity is exhausted) or stochastic, either constant or history-dependent via a logistic rule where the log-odds of killing change with accumulated non-lethal and lethal contacts. The model also includes a finite pool of targets; each successful kill reduces the number of remaining targets and proportionally decreases future encounter rates, so contacts become less frequent as targets are depleted. The outputs are per-killer sequences of lethal/non-lethal decisions with their event times, final contact histories, remaining capacities, and the trajectory of targets remaining over time.

### Analytical results

#### 1. Number of contacts per killer cell

Let $N_K$ denote the (fixed) number of killer cells and let $T(t)$ denote the number of target cells remaining at time $t$. Fix a representative killer cell $i$ and let $N_i(t)$ be its cumulative number of target-contact events up to time $t$, with $N_i(0)=0$.

Let $\mathcal{F}_t$ denote the filtration generated by the full simulator history up to time $t$ (in particular by $T(t)$ and by the contact history of cell $i$). The simulator specifies the *conditional intensity* of contacts for cell $i$ as
$$
\lambda_i(t)=r(x_i(t),y_i(t))\,f\big(T(t)\big),\qquad f(T)=\max\left(\frac{T}{T_0},f_{\min}\right).
$$
Equivalently, for small $\Delta t$,
$$
P\big(N_i(t+\Delta t)-N_i(t)=1\mid \mathcal{F}_t\big)=\lambda_i(t)\,\Delta t+o(\Delta t),\qquad
P\big(N_i(t+\Delta t)-N_i(t)\ge 2\mid \mathcal{F}_t\big)=o(\Delta t).
$$

Define the cumulative (random) intensity for that killer
$$
\Lambda_i(t)=\int_0^t \lambda_i(u)\,du.
$$

##### What can (and cannot) be derived in closed form

For general state-dependent $r(x,y)$, the marginal distribution of $N_i(t)$ does not have a simple closed form because the rate $\lambda_i(t)$ depends on the same history (through $(x_i(t),y_i(t))$) that the counting process $N_i$ generates.

##### Default constant-rate case (tractable for the simulator)

If the encounter-rate matrix is constant, $r(x,y)\equiv r_0$, then
$$
\lambda_i(t)=r_0\,f\big(T(t)\big)
$$
while the killer remains alive. In this case, conditional on the realised target trajectory $T(\cdot)$ (equivalently on the realised intensity path $\lambda_i(\cdot)$), the contact process of a fixed killer is a Poisson process with random cumulative intensity
$$
\Lambda_i(t)=\int_0^t r_0\,f\big(T(u)\big)\,du,
$$
so
$$
N_i(t)\mid T(\cdot)\sim \mathrm{Poisson}\big(\Lambda_i(t)\big).
$$
Unconditionally, $N_i(t)$ is a Poisson mixture (a Cox process):
$$
P\big(N_i(t)=k\big)=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-\Lambda_i(t)}\,\frac{\Lambda_i(t)^k}{k!}\right],\qquad
\mathbb{E}[N_i(t)]=\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_i(t)],\qquad \mathrm{Var}(N_i(t))=\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_i(t)]+\mathrm{Var}(\Lambda_i(t)).
$$

##### Closing the loop (mean-field approximation for the simulator)

If we additionally approximate the target trajectory by its mean-field evolution in the **constant-rate** setting ($r(x,y)\equiv r_0$), with **no effective capacity limit** and **constant kill probability** $p$, then the total kill intensity is
$$
\mu_{\mathrm{tot}}(t)=p\,N_K\,r_0\,\max\left(\frac{T(t)}{T_0},f_{\min}\right).
$$
Ignoring the floor ($f_{\min}=0$) gives the linear-death mean-field ODE
$$
\frac{d}{dt}T(t)= -\frac{p\,N_K\,r_0}{T_0}\,T(t),\qquad T(0)=T_0,
$$
so $T(t)/T_0\approx e^{-\gamma t}$ with $\gamma:=p\,N_K\,r_0/T_0$. Substituting this into the simulator intensity $\lambda_i(t)=r_0\,f(T(t))$ yields an explicit approximation for the cumulative contact intensity:
$$
\Lambda_i(t)\approx r_0\int_0^t e^{-\gamma u}\,du=\frac{r_0}{\gamma}\big(1-e^{-\gamma t}\big),
$$
and hence the closed-form approximation
$$
N_i(t)\approx \mathrm{Poisson}\!\left(\frac{r_0}{\gamma}\big(1-e^{-\gamma t}\big)\right).
$$
With a floor $f_{\min}>0$, the same substitution gives a piecewise explicit expression: letting $t_*:=\gamma^{-1}\log(1/f_{\min})$,
$$
\Lambda_i(t)\approx
\begin{cases}
\frac{r_0}{\gamma}\big(1-e^{-\gamma t}\big), & t\le t_*,\\
\frac{r_0}{\gamma}(1-f_{\min})+r_0 f_{\min}(t-t_*), & t> t_*.
\end{cases}
$$
This “closes the loop” in the sense that it turns the simulator-defined $\lambda_i(t)=r_0 f(T(t))$ into an explicit, directly interpretable approximation for the contact-count distribution.

#### 2. Number of kills per killer cell

Let $C(t)=N_i(t)$ denote the number of contacts experienced by a representative killer cell up to time $t$, and let
$Y(t)$ denote the number of lethal contacts (“kills”) by time $t$, with $X(t)$ the number of non-lethal contacts. In the simulator these satisfy
$$
C(t)=X(t)+Y(t),\qquad 0\le Y(t)\le K,
$$
where $K$ is the (possibly random) killing capacity of that killer.

Throughout, the contact process is as in the previous section: conditional on the realised target trajectory $T(\cdot)$ (equivalently on the realised intensity path $\lambda_i(\cdot)$),
$$
C(t)=N_i(t)\mid T(\cdot)\sim \mathrm{Poisson}\big(\Lambda_i(t)\big),\qquad \Lambda_i(t)=\int_0^t r_0\,f\big(T(u)\big)\,du.
$$
The closed-form distributions below (deterministic $p\equiv 1$ and constant $0<p<1$) correspond to the **default constant-rate case** in Section 1, i.e. $r(x,y)\equiv r_0$, for which
$$
\lambda_i(t)=r_0\,f\big(T(t)\big),\qquad \Lambda_i(t)=\int_0^t \lambda_i(u)\,du.
$$
Unconditionally (because $T(\cdot)$ is random), $C(t)$ is a Poisson mixture (Cox process). For general state-dependent $r(x,y)$, $C(t)$ does not admit a simple closed-form distribution.

##### Deterministic killing decisions ($p\equiv 1$ until capacity exhaustion)

In deterministic mode, every contact is lethal until the killer has performed $K$ kills, after which all further contacts are forced non-lethal. Therefore
$$
Y(t)=\min\{C(t),K\},\qquad X(t)=\max\{C(t)-K,0\}.
$$

Conditional on $T(\cdot)$ and $K$, the distribution follows immediately from the Poisson law for $C(t)$:
- For $k=0,1,\dots,K-1$,
$$
P\big(Y(t)=k\mid T(\cdot),K\big)=P\big(C(t)=k\mid T(\cdot)\big)=e^{-\Lambda_i(t)}\,\frac{\Lambda_i(t)^k}{k!}.
$$
- The point mass at capacity $K$ is the upper tail:
$$
P\big(Y(t)=K\mid T(\cdot),K\big)=P\big(C(t)\ge K\mid T(\cdot)\big)=1-\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}e^{-\Lambda_i(t)}\,\frac{\Lambda_i(t)^j}{j!}.
$$

If $K$ is fixed and only the intensity path is random (Cox setting), then mixing over $\Lambda(t)$ yields the explicit unconditional formulas
$$
P\big(Y(t)=k\big)=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-\Lambda_i(t)}\,\frac{\Lambda_i(t)^k}{k!}\right],\quad k=0,1,\dots,K-1,
$$
and
$$
P\big(Y(t)=K\big)=\mathbb{E}\left[1-\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}e^{-\Lambda_i(t)}\,\frac{\Lambda_i(t)^j}{j!}\right].
$$

If $K$ is itself random (heterogeneous capacities), then the unconditional distribution is obtained by mixing over both sources of randomness:
$$
P\big(Y(t)=k\big)=\mathbb{E}\Big[\,P\big(Y(t)=k\mid T(\cdot),K\big)\Big],
$$
where the expectation is over the joint law of $(T(\cdot),K)$ induced by the model.

##### Stochastic killing decisions with constant probability ($0<p<1$)

Assume each contact is lethal with a fixed probability $p$ (independent of past history), while capacity still enforces $Y(t)\le K$.

**(i) No effective capacity limit (e.g. $K=\infty$ or $K\gg Y(t)$).** Conditional on $T(\cdot)$, independent thinning of a Poisson process applies. The lethal and non-lethal contact processes are conditionally independent Poisson processes:
$$
Y(t)\mid T(\cdot)\sim \mathrm{Poisson}\big(p\Lambda_i(t)\big),\qquad X(t)\mid T(\cdot)\sim \mathrm{Poisson}\big((1-p)\Lambda_i(t)\big),
$$
and $X(t)\perp Y(t)\mid T(\cdot)$. Unconditionally, each becomes a Cox-process mixture, e.g.
$$
P\big(Y(t)=k\big)=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-p\Lambda_i(t)}\,\frac{(p\Lambda_i(t))^k}{k!}\right].
$$

**(ii) Finite capacity $K$ (hard truncation).** A convenient representation is:
1. Draw the contact count $C(t)$.
2. Given $C(t)=n$, draw the *potential* kill count $\widetilde{Y}(t)\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(n,p)$.
3. Enforce capacity by truncation: $Y(t)=\min\{\widetilde{Y}(t),K\}$.

Hence for fixed $n$ and $K$:
- For $k=0,1,\dots,K-1$,
$$
P\big(Y(t)=k\mid C(t)=n,K\big)=\binom{n}{k}p^k(1-p)^{n-k}.
$$
- For $k=K$,
$$
P\big(Y(t)=K\mid C(t)=n,K\big)=P\big(\widetilde{Y}(t)\ge K\mid C(t)=n\big)=\sum_{j=K}^{n}\binom{n}{j}p^j(1-p)^{n-j}.
$$
Mixing over $C(t)\mid T(\cdot)\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(\Lambda_i(t))$ yields $P\big(Y(t)=k\mid T(\cdot),K\big)$ as an explicit (computable) series. In this finite-capacity regime, $Y(t)$ is no longer Poisson even conditional on $T(\cdot)$ because the truncation creates a non-linear feedback once $Y(t)$ reaches $K$.

##### History-dependent killing decisions (logistic; parameters $\alpha,\beta$)

In the history-dependent stochastic mode,
$$
p(x,y)=\sigma\big(\mathrm{logit}(p_0)+\alpha x+\beta y\big),\qquad \sigma(z)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-z}}.
$$
Let $q_{x,y}(t)=P\big(X(t)=x,\,Y(t)=y\mid T(\cdot)\big)$. Conditional on $T(\cdot)$ (equivalently on $\lambda_i(\cdot)$), $(X(t),Y(t))$ is a continuous-time Markov jump process on the lattice with transitions driven by contacts:
- If $y<K$,
$$
(x,y)\to(x+1,y)\ \text{at rate }\ \lambda_i(t)\,(1-p(x,y)),\qquad
(x,y)\to(x,y+1)\ \text{at rate }\ \lambda_i(t)\,p(x,y).
$$
- If $y=K$ (capacity exhausted), only non-lethal contacts occur:
$$
(x,K)\to(x+1,K)\ \text{at rate }\ \lambda_i(t).
$$

This specification is analytically exact (given $T(\cdot)$ / $\lambda_i(\cdot)$), but it does not generally admit a closed-form marginal distribution for $Y(t)$ when $p(x,y)$ depends on $(x,y)$. In practice, one can:
- Solve the forward equations numerically on a truncated $(x,y)$ grid;
- Derive moment equations (with possible moment-closure approximations), e.g.
  $\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{E}[Y(t)\mid T(\cdot)] = \mathbb{E}[\lambda_i(t)\,p(X(t),Y(t))\,\mathbf{1}_{\{Y(t)<K\}}\mid T(\cdot)]$;
- Use simulation to estimate the induced distributions.

#### 3. Joint distribution of non-lethal and lethal contacts

The pair $(X(t),Y(t))$ can be characterised in the same conditional-on-$T(\cdot)$ framework (equivalently conditional on the per-cell intensity path $\lambda_i(\cdot)$).

**Deterministic $p\equiv 1$ with capacity $K$.** Since $Y(t)=\min\{C(t),K\}$ and $X(t)=C(t)-Y(t)$,
$$
P\big(X(t)=x,\,Y(t)=y\mid T(\cdot),K\big)=
\begin{cases}
P\big(C(t)=y\mid T(\cdot)\big), & x=0,\ 0\le y<K,\\
P\big(C(t)=x+K\mid T(\cdot)\big), & y=K,\ x\ge 0,\\
0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

**Constant $0<p<1$ without effective capacity limit.** Conditional on $\lambda(\cdot)$, independent thinning gives a product-form joint distribution:
$$
P\big(X(t)=x,\,Y(t)=y\mid T(\cdot)\big)=e^{-\Lambda_i(t)}\,\frac{((1-p)\Lambda_i(t))^x}{x!}\,\frac{(p\Lambda_i(t))^y}{y!},\qquad x,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}.
$$
Unconditionally (Cox mixture), this becomes
$$
P\big(X(t)=x,\,Y(t)=y\big)=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-\Lambda_i(t)}\,\frac{((1-p)\Lambda_i(t))^x}{x!}\,\frac{(p\Lambda_i(t))^y}{y!}\right].
$$

**Finite capacity and/or history dependence.** With truncation ($Y\le K$) and/or $p(x,y)$ varying with history, closed forms are generally unavailable; however, the Markov jump specification above provides an exact starting point for numerical forward-equation solutions and for principled approximations.

#### 4. Dynamics of remaining targets

Let $T_0$ be the initial number of targets and let $Y_{\mathrm{tot}}(t)$ be the total number of kills across all killers by time $t$. By definition,
$$
T(t)=T_0-Y_{\mathrm{tot}}(t),\qquad T(t)\in\{0,1,\dots,T_0\}.
$$

In the simulator’s default constant-rate mean-field regime (homogeneous $r(x,y)\equiv r_0$, no effective capacity limit, constant kill probability $p$), the target-count process is a **pure death process** with one-step rate
$$
T\to T-1\quad\text{at rate}\quad \mu(T)=p\,N_K\,r_0\,f(T),\qquad f(T)=\max\left(\frac{T}{T_0},f_{\min}\right).
$$
This is an exact statement about the simulator’s structure (it is just the total contact intensity $N_K r_0 f(T)$ times the kill probability $p$).

If we ignore the floor ($f_{\min}=0$), then $\mu(T)=\frac{p\,N_K\,r_0}{T_0}\,T$ and the process becomes the classical **linear death process**. In that special case,
$$
T(t)\sim \mathrm{Binomial}\big(T_0,\,e^{-\gamma t}\big),\qquad \gamma:=\frac{p\,N_K\,r_0}{T_0},
$$
so
$$
\mathbb{E}[T(t)]=T_0 e^{-\gamma t},\qquad \mathrm{Var}(T(t))=T_0 e^{-\gamma t}\big(1-e^{-\gamma t}\big).
$$

With a floor $f_{\min}>0$, the rate is piecewise: for $T>f_{\min}T_0$ it is linear in $T$, while for $T\le f_{\min}T_0$ it is constant, $\mu(T)=p\,N_K\,r_0\,f_{\min}$. The resulting death process no longer has the simple Binomial closed form, but its distribution is still computable exactly from the forward equations for a finite-state pure death chain with rates $\mu(T)$.

When killing is capacity-limited, let $K_{\mathrm{tot}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_K} K_i$ be the total available killing capacity. Then $Y_{\mathrm{tot}}(t)\le K_{\mathrm{tot}}$ and the depletion necessarily saturates at
$$
T(\infty)=T_0-\min\{T_0,K_{\mathrm{tot}}\}.
$$
The transient dynamics become coupled to the distribution of remaining capacities (killers drop out as they exhaust $K_i^{\mathrm{rem}}$), so the simple linear-death closed form no longer holds exactly; nevertheless, it remains a useful approximation when $K_{\mathrm{tot}}\gg T_0$ or on time scales before substantial capacity exhaustion.

#### 5. Summary of analytical results (Sections 1–4)

This section summarises what is **exactly specified by the simulator** versus what admits **closed-form solutions** under additional tractable assumptions.

**Exact simulator-level statements (hold for all modes).**
- For each killer $i$, the contact counting process $N_i(t)$ is defined via the conditional intensity
$$
\lambda_i(t)=r\big(x_i(t),y_i(t)\big)\,f\big(T(t)\big),\qquad f(T)=\max\left(\frac{T}{T_0},f_{\min}\right),
$$
and the cumulative intensity $\Lambda_i(t)=\int_0^t \lambda_i(u)\,du$.
- Because $\lambda_i(t)$ depends on the same history that $N_i$ generates (through $(x_i,y_i)$ and the shared target pool $T$), unconditional closed forms are generally unavailable for state-dependent $r(x,y)$ and/or history-dependent kill decisions $p(x,y)$.

**Section 1 (contacts per killer).**
- If $r(x,y)\equiv r_0$ (default constant-rate matrix), then conditional on the realised target trajectory $T(\cdot)$,
$$
N_i(t)\mid T(\cdot)\sim \mathrm{Poisson}\big(\Lambda_i(t)\big),\qquad \Lambda_i(t)=\int_0^t r_0\,f\big(T(u)\big)\,du.
$$
- Unconditionally, $N_i(t)$ is a Poisson mixture (Cox process):
$$
P\big(N_i(t)=k\big)=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-\Lambda_i(t)}\,\frac{\Lambda_i(t)^k}{k!}\right],\qquad
\mathbb{E}[N_i(t)]=\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_i(t)],\quad \mathrm{Var}(N_i(t))=\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_i(t)]+\mathrm{Var}(\Lambda_i(t)).
$$
- A mean-field “closed loop” approximation substitutes a deterministic $T(t)$ into $\Lambda_i(t)$, giving an explicit approximate Poisson law for $N_i(t)$ (with a piecewise expression when $f_{\min}>0$).

**Section 2 (kills per killer).**
- Let $C(t)=N_i(t)$, $Y(t)$ be kills, $X(t)$ non-lethal contacts, with $C(t)=X(t)+Y(t)$ and $0\le Y(t)\le K$.
- Deterministic killing ($p\equiv 1$ until capacity):
$$
Y(t)=\min\{C(t),K\},\qquad X(t)=\max\{C(t)-K,0\}.
$$
Given $T(\cdot)$ and $K$, this yields explicit point masses for $Y(t)$ via the Poisson distribution of $C(t)$.
- Constant stochastic killing ($0<p<1$): without an effective capacity limit, independent thinning gives (conditional on $T(\cdot)$)
$$
Y(t)\sim\mathrm{Poisson}\big(p\Lambda_i(t)\big),\qquad X(t)\sim\mathrm{Poisson}\big((1-p)\Lambda_i(t)\big),\qquad X\perp Y\mid T(\cdot).
$$
With finite $K$, a Binomial-with-truncation representation is available, but $Y(t)$ is no longer Poisson even conditional on $T(\cdot)$.
- History-dependent killing (logistic; $p(x,y)$): $(X(t),Y(t))$ is an exact Markov jump process given $T(\cdot)$, but closed forms are not expected; numerical forward-equation or simulation approaches are natural.

**Section 3 (joint $(X,Y)$ distribution).**
- Deterministic $p\equiv 1$ with capacity yields a degenerate joint law supported on $(x=0,0\le y<K)$ and $(y=K,x\ge 0)$.
- Constant $0<p<1$ without effective capacity limit yields a product-form joint distribution conditional on $T(\cdot)$ (independent thinning of contacts), and a Cox-mixture version unconditionally.

**Section 4 (target depletion).**
- In the default constant-rate mean-field regime (homogeneous $r_0$, no capacity limit, constant $p$), the target pool is a pure-death process
$$
T\to T-1\quad\text{at rate}\quad \mu(T)=p\,N_K\,r_0\,f(T).
$$
- If $f_{\min}=0$, this reduces to a linear death process with the Binomial closed form
$$
T(t)\sim \mathrm{Binomial}\big(T_0,e^{-\gamma t}\big),\qquad \gamma=\frac{p\,N_K\,r_0}{T_0}.
$$
- With $f_{\min}>0$, the death rates are piecewise (linear above the floor, constant below), so the simple Binomial form no longer holds, but the finite-state distribution remains computable from forward equations.
- With capacity limitation, total kills satisfy $Y_{\mathrm{tot}}(t)\le K_{\mathrm{tot}}$ and depletion saturates at $T(\infty)=T_0-\min\{T_0,K_{\mathrm{tot}}\}$.
